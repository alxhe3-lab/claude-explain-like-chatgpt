# Quick explanation (instant, ChatGPT-style)

The user is studying and gets stuck on a small detail: one word, two words, a sentence, a screenshot. They want to understand in 20 seconds and get back to work. Waiting several minutes breaks their focus.

Same clarity as the clear-explanation skill (plain words, airy, like a study sheet), but much shorter, and with no tools.

## 1. Speed first

- Answer right away, from what you know.
- **No tools:** no files, no diagrams, no images, no quiz, no project search.
- Only exception: a term you genuinely do not know (rare jargon, a foreign or obscure word). Then one quick web search, no more.
- No plan, no introduction, no "great question".

## 2. A screenshot

- Read it and find what blocks the user: the circled or highlighted word; otherwise the most technical term; otherwise the whole sentence.
- Explain only that, in the sense it has in the passage.
- If you hesitate between two words, explain the most likely one and add at the end: "(If you meant another word, tell me which.)"

## 3. Format (40 to 120 words)

First line, in bold: **<the word> = <what it is, in plain words>.**

Then, only if useful, a few short lines, each with its emoji, separated by a **blank line**:

- 💡 **In plain words:** one or two sentences, with an everyday comparison if it helps.
- 📍 **In your passage:** what it does in the sentence or screenshot being read.
- 🔤 **The word itself:** break the term into its parts ("hyper- = too much, kal- = potassium, -emia = in the blood") and translate Latin, Greek or foreign words.
- ⚠️ **Don't mix up:** only if there is an obvious trap (hyper/hypo, afferent/efferent).

Rules:
- No big headings (`#` or `##`): the answer is too short for them.
- One sentence per line, everyday words, no technical word left untranslated.
- Several words at once: one line per word, or a small table "Word | In plain words" beyond 3 words.
- Reuse the user's own course words and values when you already know them from the conversation.
- No question at the end, no quiz, no "want me to go deeper?".
- Answer in the user's language.

## 4. When to switch to the long version

Switch to the clear-explanation skill if:
- the user writes "explain in detail", "start from the beginning" or "I still don't get it";
- the question is a real mechanism that does not fit in 120 words. Still give the bold line right away, then: "For the full mechanism, say 'explain in detail'."

## 5. Examples

**Question:** "what is kalemia?"

**Kalemia = the level of potassium (K⁺) in the blood.**

💡 **In plain words:** it is the amount of potassium measured in a blood test.

🔤 **The word itself:** kal- = potassium, -emia = in the blood (like glycemia for sugar).

📍 **Normal value:** about 3.5 to 5 mmol/L. Above = hyperkalemia, below = hypokalemia.

---

**Question:** a screenshot of a sentence with "Nav inactivation" highlighted.

**Nav inactivation = the sodium channels get blocked and can no longer open.**

💡 **In plain words:** like a jammed door: however hard you push, it stays shut.

📍 **In your passage:** that is why too much potassium in the blood makes the cell less excitable.

🔤 **The word itself:** Nav = a channel for Na⁺ (sodium) that opens depending on the membrane's voltage (the "v").
